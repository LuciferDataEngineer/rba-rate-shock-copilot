# What the figures say

Snapshot taken at **9:22am, Sunday 1 November 2026 (Melbourne time)**. The next Monetary Policy Board decision is **Tuesday 3 November 2026, 2:30pm AEDT**. These are descriptions of published data, not a forecast and not financial advice.

## The cash rate

The cash rate target is **4.60%**, and it has been since it took effect on 30 September 2026. It was **3.60%** through January. Four increases of 0.25 percentage points did the whole of that rise:

| Took effect | From | To |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 4 February 2026 | 3.60% | 3.85% |
| 18 March 2026 | 3.85% | 4.10% |
| 6 May 2026 | 4.10% | 4.35% |
| 30 September 2026 | 4.35% | 4.60% |

That is **100 basis points** since the start of January. The Board’s media release is dated the meeting day, which is the business day before the new target takes effect. June and August were holds at 4.35%.

## What that costs a mortgage

The comparison is a 30-year principal-and-interest loan at the average variable rate on outstanding owner-occupier loans (RBA table F6, series FLRHOOVA, source APRA and RBA).

| | Rate | Monthly repayment on $600,000 | On $100,000 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| January 2026 | 5.5% | $3,407 | $568 |
| August 2026, the latest month published | 6.2% | $3,675 | $612 |
| Difference | +0.70 percentage points | **+$268** | **+$45** |

A further 0.25 percentage points, fully passed on from that 6.2% rate, is about **$98 a month on $600,000** and about **$16 a month per $100,000**.

The August housing-rate table does not yet include the 30 September cash-rate rise. By August the published mortgage rate was up 0.70 percentage points, against 0.75 points of cash-rate rises through May. The August Statement said banks had passed on those three earlier increases. The September increase is the one still to show up in this average.

## Prices, the target, and the real cash rate

The monthly CPI was **4.3%** higher than a year earlier in September 2026. The trimmed mean was **3.6%**. The Reserve Bank’s target is 2 to 3%.

Underlying inflation is **0.6 percentage points above the top of the target**, and **1.1 points above the 2.5% midpoint**. The quarterly CPI in table G1 is a different series: it was 3.9% over the year to the September quarter, with a trimmed mean of 3.6%. The page uses the monthly CPI, which is the 4.3% figure in the ABS release.

The real cash rate here is the 4.60% cash rate minus the 3.6% trimmed mean, which is **1.0 percentage point**. Using the monthly headline CPI instead, 4.60 minus 4.3 is 0.3 points.

## Jobs, and the share of a wage

Unemployment was **4.7%** in September, up from **4.1%** in January. The August Statement, written before that latest reading, still described the jobs market as a little tight relative to full employment.

One full-time adult’s ordinary-time earnings were **$2,083.70 a week** in the first half of 2026 (ABS average weekly earnings, seasonally adjusted). The August repayment on $600,000 is about **41%** of that one wage, up from about 38% at the January mortgage rate. A household with two incomes pays a smaller share. People who do not have a mortgage are not in this illustration.

The Reserve Bank’s own economy-wide ratio is different, and lower, because it includes every household. Scheduled housing-loan repayments were **9.4%** of household disposable income in the March quarter 2026, the latest quarter for which that ratio has been published (table E13).

## What the Bank has said

The 29 September 2026 decision raised the cash rate to 4.60% and said the decision was unanimous. The line the page quotes is the Board’s own: inflation was still too high, and a further tightening was warranted to get inflation back to target. The same statement points to higher global energy prices after the Middle East conflict widened, AI-related demand, and pressure on domestic capacity. The August Statement said the first three cash-rate rises of the year had been passed on to lending rates, and that underlying inflation was expected to be back at the middle of the target only by early 2028.

The four major banks have said they expect a rise to 4.85% on 3 November. That is their forecast, reported by Savings.com.au in late October 2026. It is not a Reserve Bank decision.
